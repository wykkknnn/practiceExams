# Question Blueprints

Use these patterns to create original university-level practice questions from course materials. Adapt wording to the discipline.

## Balanced Two-Hour Exam

- Section A: 10 short-answer questions, 2 marks each, 20 marks total.
- Section B: 4 applied problems or case questions, 10 marks each, 40 marks total.
- Section C: 2 synthesis questions, 20 marks each, 40 marks total.

Use when the course has mixed conceptual and applied material.

## Concept-Heavy Course

- Definitions and distinctions: 20%.
- Explain mechanisms, theories, or frameworks: 30%.
- Compare, critique, or interpret: 30%.
- Mini-essay or integrative case: 20%.

Use for humanities, social sciences, business theory, law-adjacent, and conceptual STEM courses.

## Quantitative or Technical Course

- Core concepts and notation: 15%.
- Procedural calculations: 35%.
- Multi-step applied problems: 35%.
- Interpretation, proof, debugging, or sensitivity analysis: 15%.

Use for mathematics, statistics, economics, finance, engineering, computer science, physics, and chemistry.

## Lab, Methods, or Data Course

- Method selection and assumptions: 20%.
- Data interpretation: 25%.
- Procedure design or troubleshooting: 25%.
- Calculation or coding reasoning: 20%.
- Ethics, limitations, or reporting: 10%.

Use when practical skills and methods are central.

## Essay or Case-Based Course

- Short concept checks: 15%.
- Case analysis: 35%.
- Comparative essay: 30%.
- Policy, design, or recommendation question: 20%.

Use for management, public policy, education, design, medicine-adjacent, and law-adjacent courses.

## Difficulty Calibration

- Easy: direct use of a definition, formula, or single concept.
- Medium: apply one concept to a new example or perform a familiar procedure.
- Hard: combine multiple topics, interpret ambiguous evidence, or justify assumptions.
- Challenge: extend the material, critique a method, or solve a novel scenario.

## Rubric Patterns

Short answer:

- 1 mark for the key concept.
- 1 mark for accurate explanation, condition, example, or implication.

Applied problem:

- Allocate marks to setup, method selection, execution, interpretation, and final answer.
- Award partial credit for correct reasoning even with arithmetic mistakes.

Essay or synthesis:

- Thesis or direct answer.
- Correct use of course concepts.
- Evidence or examples from materials.
- Depth of analysis.
- Limitations, counterarguments, or assumptions.
- Clarity and structure.
